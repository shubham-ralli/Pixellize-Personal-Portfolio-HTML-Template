Writing CSS that scales with your team

Alex CarterPublished on March 14, 2026

CSS is easy to write and hard to maintain. On a solo project you can get away with almost anything, but the moment a second developer joins, small inconsistencies start to compound into real problems. Here are the patterns I rely on to keep stylesheets predictable as a team and a codebase grow.

Start with design tokens

Before writing a single component, define your primitives: colors, spacing, typography and radii as CSS custom properties on :root. Everything downstream references these tokens instead of hard-coded values, so a theme change is a one-line edit rather than a find-and-replace marathon.

“The best CSS architecture is the one your whole team can follow without thinking about it.”

Keep specificity flat

Deeply nested selectors are the single biggest source of CSS pain. Favor a single class per component and avoid chaining selectors more than one level deep. When specificity stays flat, overriding styles is trivial and you almost never reach for !important.

A quick checklist

  • One class, one responsibility.
  • No IDs for styling.
  • Co-locate component styles with the component.
  • Name by intent, not by appearance (.btn-primary, not .btn-green).

Document the system

A design system that lives only in one person's head isn't a system. Even a simple style guide page — showing your buttons, forms and spacing scale — saves hours of back-and-forth and keeps new contributors on the rails.

Adopt these habits early and your stylesheet stays an asset instead of slowly becoming the thing nobody wants to touch. For more, read the rest of the articles on the blog.
